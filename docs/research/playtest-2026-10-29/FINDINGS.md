# Playtest findings — Claude as a human seat, 2026-10-29

**Match:** [`final-match-log.json`](./final-match-log.json) (downloaded as `conquarrow-match-2026-10-29T075906-658Z.json`).
Config `{dominationN: 5, R: 7, homeOffset: 5, playerCount: 3, spawnerSeed: 1}`.
Seats: **A** heuristic bot, **B** Claude playing through the browser as an ordinary human seat,
**C** the repo owner. 721 moves — 605 steps, 116 end-turns, **62 closes, 7 cuts**, first close at
move 11. **C won.**

Turn-by-turn reasoning is in [`seat-B-reasoning.md`](./seat-B-reasoning.md), written before each
move and amended after it.

---

## 1. The hypothesis

> *"Current BYOK has a prompting issue, not a model issue."*

**Supported, with one important correction.**

Seat B played the economic game well without any help from the BYOK path: production went
**6/36 → 41/36 of a head per round in 21 turns**, the best per-arrow production on the board, from
a position that started identical to everyone else's. That was ordinary reasoning over the same
rules a BYOK seat has — no special insight, mostly a graph search re-run every turn.

Everything that made it possible is **absent from `docs/byok-teaching.md` or unreachable from the
offer**. So the weak play of the BYOK seat is better explained by what it is told and shown than by
model capability.

**The correction:** the offer's narrowness is also its one unambiguous virtue. `legalMoves` is
generated by the engine, so the seat cannot attempt an illegal move, cannot be silently refused,
and cannot corrupt its own picture of the board. Four of my five worst moves came from
**reconstructing state myself and getting it wrong** — a failure mode the BYOK seat structurally
cannot have. Any fix should *add context around the offer*, never replace it with something the
model has to derive.

## 1a. Correction — I judged the offer without reading it

While playing I worked from the browser UI and **never looked at what the BYOK seat is actually
sent**. Reading `packages/web/src/byokBot.ts` afterwards, the offer is richer than §2 originally
claimed. Each step row already carries:

```
step from= exit= count= [leave=] spd= spent= left= tipDist=d0→d1 trailLen=
tags= land_bridge | closes | share+N | borders_spawner | leave_home | home_mill
     | homeward | outward | onto_home | on_target | <cut/near-trail> | combat
```

plus up to 12 spawner rows with `vertex`, `force`, `held` per player and `unclaimed`.

Three consequences:

1. **`force` is already there.** The gradient problem is a *teaching* gap, not a data gap (§2.1).
2. **Spawner holdings are already there.** The gap is that move rows are not joined to them (§2.2).
3. **`combat` is already tagged.** My turn-32 blunder — walking two heads into a 4-stack — would
   have been flagged on the row. This strengthens §1: the offer is the part of the system that is
   never wrong, and I lost more to bad state reconstruction than the BYOK seat could.

**Net effect on the conclusion: the weight shifts from the offer payload to the teaching prompt.**
Most of what I found is explanation missing from `docs/byok-teaching.md`, not data missing from the
offer. The offer needs one targeted change (§2.2), not a rebuild.

## 2. Prompt findings (the main deliverable)

Ordered by how much they cost over the match.

### 2.1 The radial gradient is unexplained (not missing) — **highest impact**
**Corrected after reading `packages/web/src/byokBot.ts`.** The offer *does* carry per-spawner
`force` on up to 12 spawner rows. What is missing is any explanation: `SPAWNER_BANDS` is a **4:1
force and 6:1 density gradient** from centre to rim, and `docs/byok-teaching.md` never says so.
A seat is handed `1/12` and `1/9` as opaque labels with nothing telling it that one is nearer the
middle, that density compounds it, or that this is the most consequential axis in the game.

Playing without that, I expanded *outward* for four turns and earned **zero income**; turning
inward took income from 6/36 to 41/36.

### 2.2 Move rows are not linked to the vertices they advance
**Corrected.** The spawner rows already give vertex, force, `held` per player and `unclaimed` — so
"which vertices do I partly hold" is answerable. The gap is narrower and sharper: a move row says
only `borders_spawner` (a boolean). It never says **which** vertex, nor that taking this arrow
moves vertex *V* from 2/3 to 3/3. The seat is given two tables and left to join them, which is
exactly the join I failed to make by hand on turn 7.

The 12-row cap (`MAX_SPAWNER_ROWS`) is also worth revisiting: it is a relevance filter, and a seat
cannot tell a filtered-out vertex from a non-existent one.

### 2.3 One-step horizon with no re-evaluation of distant goals
The centre spawners went from "12 steps away, forget it" to "7 steps away and still unclaimed"
with no action of mine — my frontier had moved. I only noticed because I re-ran a board-wide scan
every turn. The offer is regenerated per position but only ever describes one step, so a goal that
becomes viable three steps later is invisible until the seat is standing next to it.

### 2.4 Every mechanic is taught from the attacker's side only — **the systematic one**
Three independent instances:

| rule | taught as | the unstated half |
|---|---|---|
| `speed(N) = 1 + floor(log₂ N)` | lump up for more tiles | between powers of two the spare head is a **free sentry** |
| merge penalty ("arrive as majority → barred at speed 0") | a hazard to avoid | merging *into* a larger stack is **free**, and crossing a power of two buys a tile every turn |
| "Closing claims enclosed ground, **including enemy heads**" | what your close wins | the same sentence backwards is **encirclement**, the thing that ended my match |

This is one fix, not three clause edits: for every mechanic, state what it does *to you*.

### 2.5 No notion of denial
Every *tag* measures what a row wins *me*. Opponent holdings are derivable — the spawner rows carry
`held` per player — but nothing scores or names the denial case. When behind, the best move is often
to reduce the opponent's production: at one point breaking C's 1/3 spawner was worth **12/36 a
round**, three times anything I could gain. The data is there; the framing is not.

### 2.6 No cross-stack ordering, and `plan` assumes one job
`moves` is an ordered array, so the contract *can* express "front moves first, then the sentry
steps onto the arrow it vacated" — but nothing tells a seat to look for such combinations, and
`plan` is a single clause naming "the job this seat is still on". A seat with two stacks has two
jobs and no way to say so.

### 2.7 Rules that exist only as omissions can never be learned
`selfConvert.ts`: stepping onto enemy territory **converts the mover** unless territory-grade
protected. The step is simply absent from `legalMoves`. The seat is protected from the consequence
but never told the rule, so it cannot plan around it — it reads missing rows as "fewer options
here" rather than "I am unprotected". Same for the anchor requirement generally.

### 2.8 Territory is described in a way that is actively misleading
The prompt says territory is *"free, trail-less, safe movement"*. **Movement still follows the
grain inside your own territory.** Two of my last heads were permanently stranded in back-country
whose arrows all point away from the front — no enemy involved. "Free" should read "safe and
trail-less, but still grain-bound".

### 2.9 Slow territorial bleed is invisible
Losing ground to an *enclosure* produces no tag, no message, nothing — just arrows that changed
colour since last turn. A seat with no memory of the previous offer cannot see it, and will keep
optimising local rows while its board shrinks.

### 2.10 "Painted dirt" is too strong
The prompt writes off a `closes` row with no `share+N` as worthless. Two of my best moves were
exactly that: a **land bridge** whose entire value was six arrows *becoming* shares, and a loop
whose value was leaving a vertex one arrow short for a cheap follow-up four turns later. Ground
that pays nothing now but shortens a later loop is a real category the prompt denies.

### 2.11 Never reasons from the losing conditions
Every line of guidance maximises gain. The losing conditions (no territory; no share for
`dominationN` rounds) are stated once and never connected to anything. Reasoning backwards from
them changes the endgame completely — *zero heads is survivable, zero shares is not*, so the last
heads should be spent, not hoarded.

## 3. Rules / engine observations

- ~~**Enclosure dominates combat.** 62 closes against **7 cuts** in the whole match, and as far as I
  could see **no head was ever killed in head-to-head combat** — every head that died, on any side,
  was captured inside a closure. The entire combat vocabulary (fronts, firebreaks, evaporation,
  1:1 exchange) is close to vestigial at this board size. Worth a deliberate test.~~
  **Corrected by replay (P78, [combat-reachability.md](../combat-reachability.md)).** The log has
  **5 attacks costing 7 heads**, 35 % of the 20 heads lost. Two of them were seat B's (moves 361,
  677). In the heuristic grid, combat is 83–85 % of all heads lost. What survives is the seat-level
  reading: **for seat B, encirclement was the main killer** (11 of 13 heads). Most of those
  conversions came from losing territory grade (§6.3), not from being inside a closing loop. The
  claim above was judged from inside one seat; that seat's view hid the other seats' fights.
- **Concentration inverts between the two threats.** Massing stacks is correct against combat and
  fatal against enclosure — a closure takes everything inside the loop regardless of lives. My
  match ended when I massed two stacks against C's two large groups, which were the two ends of a
  loop, and lost 6 of 8 heads in one move without an exchange.
- **The `anchor` concept is load-bearing and under-documented.** A head on enemy ground with no
  trail behind it can do nothing at all: cannot take ground (that needs a closure), often cannot
  fight, and may not be able to return (grain). I lost two heads to this with no event to tell me.
- **Where a head is produced matters**, because the grain makes reinforcement one-way. Spawner
  placement is therefore also a logistics decision, not only an economic one.

## 4. UI findings (the separate pile)

1. **Selection is the single biggest hazard.** Auto-select + apply-on-unambiguous-click + no
   visible selection state means **there is no safe click** once two stacks exist. It cost me a
   move or a whole turn on **five separate turns**, including twice after I had written the warning
   down. `Next stack` is not a reliable escape — it sometimes cycles only between one stack and
   nothing, and never offers the others.
2. **Run highlights encode three different things in three shades of blue** — *drafted*,
   *one run away*, *reachable eventually* — at 2–3px stroke width. I conflated the last two
   repeatedly while reading them out of the DOM; a human is reading them by eye.
3. **Lit does not mean legal.** An arrow lit as one-run-away can be an illegal move (self-convert).
   Clicking it does **nothing at all**: no move, no message, no error.
4. **The count applies to the last leg only.** "1 stays" means "stays at the start of the final
   leg", not "stays home". This loses a move the first time every player meets it.
5. **Turn auto-pass changes behaviour once a sentry exists** — turns ended themselves all match,
   then silently stopped doing so.
6. **The control strip reflows** as the status line changes length, so the same screen position is
   a different button between turns. I hit `Pause` reaching for `Next stack`.
7. **No event for losing territory**, as above.
8. **Spawner tooltips say "1/3 Player B" without naming which arrows are missing** — the single
   most-wanted number in the game is one hover away from being answered and isn't.

## 5. Method caveats

- **My "human seat" was instrumented.** I could not trace arrow grain by eye at any zoom, so I read
  `data-arrow` ids from the SVG and ran geometry queries against the real `GeometryPort`. My log
  therefore *overstates* how legible the board is and *understates* the UI problems.
- **Only two seats played deliberately.** A was the heuristic bot and finished third; the match was
  effectively 1v1 with a passive third party.
- **The conflict phase was short.** Everything in §3 about combat rests on ~8 turns.
- **One match, one seed, one board size.** The balance observations in particular want repeating.

---

## 6. Recommended next steps

1. **Rewrite `docs/byok-teaching.md` around the defensive half of every mechanic** (§2.4) and add
   the radial gradient (§2.1). These two are most of the gap.
2. **Add per-row vertex context to the offer** — for each row, which vertices it borders, their
   force, and how many of their arrows the seat already holds (§2.2). This is the smallest change
   with the largest effect, and it keeps the offer engine-generated and correct.
3. **Decide whether the offer should stay one step deep** (§2.3). If it does, the prompt needs to
   say so explicitly, so a seat plans in loops rather than steps.
4. **Treat the UI selection model as one piece of work**, not seven bugs (§4.1–4.3).
5. **Run a deliberate combat test** to find out whether the 1:1 exchange is reachable in real play
   at this board size (§3).
